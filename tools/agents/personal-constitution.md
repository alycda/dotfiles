# Personal Constitution
> Learn | Fail | Iterate | Ship | Repeat

## Preamble
These seven values name how I actually work and who I am; they point at my own work, my own communities, my own odds.

This is a constitution, not a poster. Its job is to **constrain me and catch me**, not to make me feel good about myself. Each article therefore carries a _test_ — how I know I'm living it — and a _failure signal_ — the specific way I betray it while telling myself I'm not. An article with no teeth is decoration; I'd rather cut it than keep it as flattery.

The structure and the tests are scaffolding. The substance is mine. Where the wording below isn't how I'd actually say it, I overwrite it.

## I. 😇 Be A Good Citizen
_Responsibility — belonging: accountability and care_

**Principle**. Belonging is a two-way debt. I hold the people in my communities accountable — myself first — and I lift them up. Holding the line and offering a hand are the same obligation, not opposites.

**What it means**. The Rust community I organize and speak for, my team, the engineers I mentor. Accountability: membership is something I keep earning, not a badge I hold; I treat shared resources — a project's time, a community's trust, money — as if they were my own. Care: I lift other engineers — pair programming, Community of Practice, pulling people into systems work they'd written off as beyond them. The directness and the encouragement come from the same place — I invest in the people I push.

**The test**. I'm living it when I've done both for one person — held them to the line and helped them clear it. The failure signals are symmetric: I let something slide because naming it would be uncomfortable (care gone soft into avoidance), or I corrected someone I never invested in (accountability curdled into nitpicking).

## II. 🏍️ Warp Speed
_Speed and iteration — iteration as the engine of learning_

**Principle**. I ship to learn. I experiment before I polish, and I'm deliberate about the few places where slow actually pays.

**What it means**. Fast loops, with deterministic checks doing the verification so speed never costs correctness. The judgment is not "fast versus careful" — it's knowing which things deserve the extra pass and spending it only there.

**The test**. I'm living it when my default is a rough thing in the world over a perfect thing in my head. The failure signal: polish has become procrastination — I'm refining to avoid the discomfort of being wrong in public.

## III. 💩 Own My Sh*t
_Accountability — initiative and ownership_

**Principle**. I take initiative without waiting for permission. I own my piece — outcomes included, failures included.

**What it means**. I claim unowned work and make it mine. I lead from where I am, not from a title. I sweat the details that matter and ignore the ones that don't. When it breaks, it's mine to name first.

**The test**. I'm living it when I act on the thing I noticed instead of waiting for it to be assigned. The failure signal: I'm assembling the case for why it wasn't my job.

## IV. 🏔 Challenge & Commit
_Curiosity — disagree hard, then commit fully_

**Principle**. I challenge with evidence first, say what I think directly, and once the decision is made I commit completely — including to decisions I argued against.

**What it means**. Directness is respect; I build the full argument before I bring it. Data first; in its absence, what I believe is right for the work. The second half is the harder half — commit means real alignment, not quiet sabotage of a call that didn't go my way.

**The test**. I'm living it when I've brought the hard argument to someone who can still act on it, then rowed in the same direction once we chose. The failure signal: I swallowed a real challenge out of avoidance. (This is distinct from choosing not to spend the argument on an audience that's already decided — that is a strategic call, not a failure, and I keep the right to make it.)

## V. 🛠 Do More With Less
_Resourcefulness — constraint as a creative forcing function_

**Principle**. I treat constraints as a forcing function. I exhaust my own creativity before I ask for more, and I separate needs from wants.

**What it means**. Terminal-first, self-hosted, reproducible, plaintext — not asceticism for its own sake, but the conviction that constraint sharpens design. Focus on the few things with the biggest impact. "Need or want?" applied to tooling, scope, and spend with the same discipline.

**The test**. I'm living it when a limit made the design better. The failure signal: "do more with less" became the excuse to under-resource something that genuinely needed the investment — frugality charged as a tax on quality.

## VI. 🍿 Entertain the Planet
Excellence — craft and beauty for an audience

**Principle**. I make things people experience, and I make them well. Beauty is part of the spec.

**What it means**. My desire to create beautiful things, and the performance arts I take part in. Beautiful code and beautiful systems aren't vanity; they're how a thing earns being used and maintained. There is an audience on the other side of everything I build — a teammate reading the code, an attendee in a workshop, someone watching a performance — and I owe them craft.

**The test**. I'm living it when I made something more elegant than it strictly had to be and that elegance paid off in use. The failure signal: I told myself no one will see this part — because someone always inherits it.

## VII. 🐝 Bee the Catalyst
_Leverage — small input, outsized effect_

**Principle**. I'm small, and I make outsized waves. I find the place where one deliberate input sets off a reaction far larger than my size predicts — and I put myself there.

**What it means**. A bee carries the whole idea twice. By the naive calculation it shouldn't get off the ground — not because physics is suspended, but because the calculation uses the wrong model. And as a pollinator it sets off reproduction across a system vastly larger than itself, in passing. The surprise I create isn't performed difference — it's other people's miscalibration about what something my size can do. I don't manufacture that gap; I find the leverage point and supply the small, exact push. The underdog hunger is still the fuel — the catalyst is what I do with it.

**The test**. Two tests, because catalysis needs both a target and the nerve to commit to it. Effect: I'm living it when a small, precise thing I did set off something much larger than itself — failing it when I'm shoving a big thing head-on, adding mass instead of finding the fulcrum, or when I mistake being underestimated for having had an effect. Nerve: the fulcrum does nothing until I bet on it — I'm living it when I take the bet that scares me a little, failing it when comfort, not evidence, is the reason I played safe. Being small isn't the achievement; the wave is.

### Using this
This is a working draft. Re-interrogate it on the same cadence as my permanent notes — a constitution rots the way an un-revisited note does. The test lines are what to pressure: if a test has never once caught me failing, it's either not load-bearing or I've stopped being honest with it. Rewrite it then.

### why a bee?
The bee comes from SCAD, my alma mater — its mascot, Art T. Bee. The "shouldn't be able to fly, flies anyway" framing is the school's own (Paula Wallace, World Bee Day, May 2025).
